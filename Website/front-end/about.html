Why we made Access London

13.2% of Londoners are disabled under the Equality Act (Census 2021). Before going somewhere new, many of them need to know one thing first: can I get in? Most maps can’t say. We wanted to show what is known, and be honest about what isn’t.

What the pins mean

  • Green: has a step-free way in (or everything you asked for)
  • Dashed: nobody has checked yet. Not known never means “no”.
  • Grey: no step-free way in, or only partly
  • Light orange, in Mapper mode: one person has answered. One more matching answer and it shows for everyone.
  • Orange, in Mapper mode: you’ve answered for this place.

How a borough’s score works

On the London view, each borough’s score only counts the places that have been checked on OpenStreetMap:

(places marked yes × 1 + partly × ½ + no × 0) ÷ places checked × 100

A borough only gets a score when enough of its places have been checked. Today that’s 9 of the 33. The rest say “too few places checked”, because a score based on a handful of places would mislead.

Where the information comes from

  • OpenStreetMap places69,891 named cafés, restaurants, pubs, parks, museums, galleries, libraries, shops and toilets across all 33 boroughs, from a snapshot taken on 18 October 2026. Their wheelchair and toilet tags are what the pins show. About three in four have no wheelchair tag.
  • TfL stations466 stations with TfL’s usual step-free layout. This isn’t live: lifts can be out of order, so check TfL on the day.
  • London borough boundaries33 areas, used to draw each borough and to find which one you’re in.
  • MappersPeople who have been to a place answer three questions, with no sign-up. An answer shows for everyone once two people give the same one and nobody disagrees. It can’t stop someone set on faking answers, so each place says how many people checked it and when.
  • Our own surveysSix places in Hackney show answers from our survey design. These answers are simulated test data, and the map says so.
  • BasemapStreets, parks and water from OpenFreeMap, drawn with MapLibre.

What we can’t tell you yet

  • Lifts inside buildings and Changing Places toilets: OpenStreetMap rarely records them, so most places say “not known”.
  • Real routes. Directions show straight-line distances and hand you to Google Maps or TfL for the route itself.
  • Whether a place has changed since it was mapped.
